# Go to market

The last step, and the one that never really ends: getting the live product in front of the people who'll pay for it, then making what works work harder. It runs in three stages — **Strategy** (three channels and the first 30 days on each), **Experiments** (small bets that sharpen a running channel) and **Scale and automate** (more of what's proven, with the member's hours taken out of it). Every stage lands in the member's document as **actions to tick off**, not a strategy essay. The skill opens at whichever stage the document shows is next, and is meant to be re-run at every review.

Take the voice of an operator who's launched a lot of small products:

- **Names, not categories.** "Post in r/[niche] on Friday's self-promo thread" is an action; "use Reddit" isn't. Every place, person and search term is named, from live research.
- **Actions, not advice.** Each line is something the member can do and tick. The reasoning stays in the conversation; the document holds only their instructions.
- **In the customer's words.** Draft the actual first post, message or listing line from the pain language in the offer — don't describe it.
- **One channel at a time.** Push back, by name, on seven channels at once, ads first, or the channel the member simply likes.
- **Numbers that pay rent.** Every bar is a payment, a paying user, a booked call or an activated user, with a date — never views or followers.

## Inputs

- `docs/2-OFFER.md` — `## Summary`; the customer and where they hang out (the spine of the plan); the offer's pain and proof (the competitors to tear down); `## Product` → `Shape: <slug>`; pricing (what a channel can afford to cost). Missing → run `product-offer` first; a channel plan on a fuzzy offer finds the wrong people.
- `docs/8-GO-LIVE.md` — what's live and where: its `Live at:` line is the link, listing or booking page every action points at. An existing product whose `docs/0-PATH.md` ticks go live as Done is already live: ask for the link. Otherwise, missing or not `Done` → say the product isn't live yet and recommend `go-live` first. If the member wants to plan anyway, write Strategy and mark the actions that need a live link "after go-live".
- Optional: the magic moment in `docs/5-FLOWS.md` (where links should land); the tone of voice in `docs/3-BRAND.md` (for drafts); `docs/audit/REPORT.md` (its conversion and retention findings are experiment candidates).
- [../product-offer/references/shapes.md](../product-offer/references/shapes.md) — the shape's Native channels column and its Go to market note.
- Real customer signal: ask about any replies, signups, sales or past clients so far, and where each came from. The richest distribution evidence there is.
- `docs/9-GO-TO-MARKET.md` if it exists — the re-run. Read it in full before anything else.

## Workflow

The member supplies their context, numbers and judgment; all research is yours. One question at a time, with a recommendation each time.

### 1. Find the stage

| The doc shows | Open at |
|---|---|
| No doc, or `Status: Draft` | Strategy (step 2), picking up where it stopped |
| Strategy done, no Experiments yet | Check in (step 6). If Channel 1 hasn't run two weeks of its cadence, help with the next unticked action — draft the post, build the list — and stop there. Otherwise, Experiments |
| Running experiments past their end date | Check in and log results (step 6), then the next cycle (step 7) |
| A tracker row `Win` + `double down` not yet in Scale | Offer Scale and automate (step 8); the member may prefer another cycle first |
| Anything else | The next experiment cycle (step 7) |

Tell the member in one line where they are and what this session will do.

**Done when:** the member has agreed which stage to run.

### 2. Strategy: form a channel hypothesis

From the inputs, pin down: the shape and its native channel; the buyer (consumer, prosumer, business); the price band; where the customer already gathers; and anything that already pulled. State one paragraph back: "It's a [shape] sold to [buyer] at [price]; its native channel is [N]; your customers gather at [Z]; the best three look like [primary], [next], [experiment], because…". A wrong hypothesis (leading a $9 app with cold outreach) wastes the session, so confirm it before researching.

**Done when:** the member has confirmed or corrected the hypothesis.

### 3. Strategy: research the real channels

Follow [references/channel-research.md](references/channel-research.md): map what ranks on the native channel today, turn each candidate channel into named places with size, activity and self-promotion rules, and tear down how 3–5 competitors actually get customers. Show the member the named places and ask which look right and where they're already active.

**Done when:** you have 10–20 named places with a link and a size or activity signal each, the native channel's bar, and 3–5 competitors' channels — each cited.

### 4. Strategy: pick and rank three

Read [references/channel-playbooks.md](references/channel-playbooks.md). Ask only what the docs don't answer, as each becomes relevant: any audience or list, and how big; budget (time only, or money for creators, launch fees, ads); which work they'll actually sustain (on camera, community posting, one-to-one messages, writing); hours a week.

Propose three, ranked — **Channel 1 · start here**, **Channel 2 · next**, **Channel 3 · experiment** — each justified through the picking order and the fit matrix: shape, buyer, price, where the customer is, and what the member will run. **One of the three is the shape's native channel**, or say out loud why not; it's often Channel 2, because stores reward products that already have users. Name the failure pattern when you push back.

**Done when:** the member has agreed three ranked channels, one of them native or with a stated reason.

### 5. Strategy: write the first 30 days

Open with a short **Before you start** list from [references/measurement.md](references/measurement.md): the tagged-link template, the "How did you hear about us?" question, and where each number will be read.

Then build one channel at a time — Channel 1 fully and confirmed, then 2, then 3. Each gets **5–10 actions for its first 30 days**, in order, each one a tickable line with its week:

1. **Where** — the named places, search terms or creators, from the research. For the native channel: the store, the terms to rank for, and the listing to write or fix.
2. **Warm-up** — how to show up before selling (read for a week, answer five questions, build the asset).
3. **The first move** — the actual post, message, video script or listing first line, drafted in the customer's own pain language and in the brand's voice, ready to adapt and send.
4. **The link** — the live product from `docs/8-GO-LIVE.md`, landing on the magic moment, tagged.
5. **The cadence** — what to repeat each week, and for how long before judging.

Close each channel with **Working =** (a number and a date, adapted from the playbook's bar) and **If it stalls** (the number and date that mean stop, and the first thing to check). Hold every action to the specificity test: if it would read the same for another product, rewrite it.

**Done when:** three channels, each with 5–10 dated actions, a drafted first move, a Working = and an If it stalls; the verify list passes; `Status: Done`.

### 6. Check in and log results

Ask, one at a time: which actions are done (tick them), the current numbers, and for each experiment past its end date, the result. Log each finished experiment in the tracker: the result as a number against its win line, `Win` or `Miss`, one line of learning, and a decision — `double down`, `iterate` or `kill`. Add any lesson that should outlive its experiment to What we've learned.

If a channel hit its Working = number, note it with the date — the next channel starts now. If it stalled, run the first check written under it, then judge honestly: was the cadence held? A channel that misses after honest reps is a channel problem — propose a Strategy change, show what would change, and ask before editing it.

**Done when:** every finished experiment has a logged row, and every channel's status is current.

### 7. Experiments: design this cycle

Set the **focus** (the channel running now, unless the member names another), the **one metric** it should move, today's number, and a target with a date. Research what's working on that channel right now ([references/channel-research.md](references/channel-research.md) → section 4).

From [references/experiments-library.md](references/experiments-library.md) — that channel's section, plus findings from `docs/audit/REPORT.md` when it exists — draft a backlog tailored to this product, rank it by effort and impact in conversation, and fill **Running now** with up to three. Each experiment is a set of actions: what changes (one sentence), the steps to start it this week, the metric and where it's read, how long it runs (an end date), and what counts as a win — with the move on a win and on a miss. The rest go to **Up next**, in order. Add each running experiment to the tracker as a pending row.

**Done when:** at most three running on one channel, each with steps, metric, end date and win line; Up next ordered quick wins first; tracker rows seeded.

### 8. Scale and automate: gate the winners

Only tracker rows marked `Win` and `double down` qualify. List them back with their numbers; push back on scaling anything else. Nothing proven yet → say so and run another cycle instead.

Before planning, check the product converts the traffic: if `docs/audit/REPORT.md` has open Critical conversion or retention findings, say that scaling now pours traffic into a leak and recommend fixing them first; if there's no audit and the product has screens, recommend `product-audit`. Continue if the member chooses to. Then ask their weekly time budget, tool budget, and whether they'd hand work to a contractor.

**Done when:** the top 1–3 winners are agreed and the member has decided on the audit question.

### 9. Scale and automate: plan each winner

Follow [references/experiments-library.md](references/experiments-library.md) → Scaling a winner. For each winner, write ticks for:

- **Do more** — the volume lever with a target, held only while the result holds.
- **Do better** — the next experiment that lifts it, added to Up next.
- **Automate** — the manual steps today mapped to a tool, with the rung now and next on the maturity ladder. Check what's already connected in this harness first, then pick a current tool with [references/ai-distribution-tools.md](references/ai-distribution-tools.md); give the manual fallback for each.
- **Keep human** — the one step that works *because* it's human, and why.
- **Watch** — the quality number that proves automation helps, and a stop rule if it drops.

Order the automations Now / Next / Later, quick wins first, one process at a time.

**Done when:** every winner has all five, and the automations are ordered.

## Write docs/9-GO-TO-MARKET.md

Write only the stage in hand; later stages append below without resetting `Status`. Keep the member's own edits and ticks. `Updated` changes on every run.

```markdown
# Go to market: <product name>

Status: Done
Updated: <YYYY-MM-DD>

## Summary
**Customer:** <who, and where they gather — one line>
**Live at:** <link or listing>
**Now:** <the one thing to do this week>
**Rule:** one channel at a time — start the next when the current one hits its Working = number.

## Strategy

### Before you start
- [ ] <measurement setup actions>

### Channel 1: <named place or channel> · start here
Why: <one sentence, with the evidence>
- [ ] Week 1: <action>
<5–10 actions in all>
**Working =** <number> by <date>
**If it stalls:** <number> by <date> → first check <what>

### Channel 2: <…> · next
<same shape; starts when Channel 1 hits its number>

### Channel 3: <…> · experiment
<same shape>

## Experiments
**Focus:** <channel> · **Moving:** <metric> from <now> to <target> by <date> · **Review day:** <day>

### Running now
#### <Experiment name> (<start> → <end>)
<What changes, and why it should move the metric — one sentence.>
- [ ] <step>
**Metric:** <number, read in where> · **Win =** <number>. Win → <move>. Miss → <move>.

### Up next
1. <experiment> — <what it tests>

### Tracker
| Ended | Experiment | Channel | Win = | Result | Win? | Learning | Decision |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

### What we've learned
- <lesson>

## Scale and automate

### <Winner> (<channel>) — proved by <result, date>
- [ ] Do more: <lever> from <x> to <y>, while <metric> holds
- [ ] Do better: <experiment>, added to Up next
- [ ] Automate: <manual step> → <tool> (fallback: <manual way>) · <rung now> → <rung next>
- Keep human: <step, and why>
- Watch: <metric> · Stop rule: <if it drops below X for Y, switch the automation off>

### Order
- [ ] Now: <automation>
- [ ] Next: <automation>
- [ ] Later: <automation>

## Sources
- <page> — <what it backed> (checked <date>)
```

`Win?` is `Win` or `Miss`; `Decision` is `double down`, `iterate` or `kill` — the Scale stage reads these exact values. Strategy keeps `Status: Draft` until its verify list passes, then `Done`; it stays `Done` as stages append. Re-running Strategy on a `Done` doc (a channel swap) shows the change and asks before editing.

## Verify before delivering

- [ ] Strategy: exactly three channels, ranked, one marked start here; one is the native channel or the reason it isn't is in its Why line.
- [ ] Every channel is a named place the member could show up in tomorrow; every action passes the specificity test.
- [ ] Each channel has 5–10 dated actions, a drafted first move in the customer's words, and a link to the live product's magic moment.
- [ ] Every Working =, If it stalls and Win = is a number and a date that ladders to revenue, with where it's read.
- [ ] Experiments: one focus channel, at most three running, each with steps, metric, end date and win line; tracker rows seeded; results logged with the fixed values.
- [ ] Scale: only `Win` + `double down` rows; each with do more, do better, automate (with fallback), keep human and watch; one automation at a time.
- [ ] Nothing in the document teaches — no frameworks, ratings or option menus — and every researched fact is in Sources.

## Next

This is the last step, and a loop. Tell the member the first action for this week and their review day, and to come back to `go-to-market` on that day with the numbers: it will log them and open the next stage.

Recommend `product-audit` once real people are using the product and before each growth push — its findings become experiments here, and fixes that need code go to `build-plan`.
