# Experiments library

Proven, channel-by-channel experiments to tailor for the Experiments stage, plus the rules for scaling a winner. Every experiment here is a shape to fill with this product's words, places and numbers — never paste one as written. Live research ([channel-research.md](channel-research.md) → section 4) makes each one current.

Founder figures are self-reported in Starter Story interviews, paraphrased, compiled June 2026.

## The method

- **The loop:** run small → measure against the win line → decide → log. The decision is one of three: **double down** (beat the line), **iterate** (close — change one thing, rerun), **kill** (missed — try a different experiment).
- **Rank by effort and impact** (Low / Medium / High each). Quick wins (high impact, low effort) first; then big bets (high impact, high effort) one at a time; filler (low/low) only when blocked; never high effort, low impact. The ratings are for the conversation — the member's doc gets only the order.
- **Three at a time, one focus channel**, in one- or two-week cycles. More and nobody can tell what moved the number.
- **Measure what counts:** signups, trials, paying users, booked calls, cost per customer — as far down the funnel as can be read ([measurement.md](measurement.md)). A win measured in views or likes gets redesigned.
- **A dead channel isn't an experiment problem.** If a channel misses its line after honest reps at the planned cadence, go back to Strategy and change the channel.

## How a card reads

**Name** · effort · impact · ⭐ for a quick win. **Test** — what changes. **Win =** — the number and timeframe. **Steps** — what to do this week. **Then** — the move on a win and on a miss. *Example* — a founder who ran a version of it. Square brackets are for this product's specifics.

## 1. Short-form content

**Hook Test** · Low · High · ⭐
- **Test:** the same 15–30s demo posted 5 times, one a day, each with a different opening line (a pain, a result, a "POV…") in the customer's words.
- **Win =** one hook clears 50% 3-second retention and 2× the median saves within 5 days.
- **Then:** win → it's the default opener; rerun with 5 fresh hooks. All miss → it's the format, not the hook (Format Clone).
- *Prayer Lock (Mal Baron, reports $20K/mo): recorded ~40 takes until one hook format clicked.*

**Format Clone** · Low · High · ⭐
- **Test:** find the top video in the niche hashtag (sort by most-liked), copy its hook, beats, length and call to action, remake it with the product.
- **Win =** 10K organic views with no boost within 2 weeks.
- **Then:** win → 3 more in that format. Miss → clone a different proven format, not a fresh idea.
- *PuffCount (Steven Cravada, reports $40K/mo): copied a 3.7M-like format; 50M+ organic views.*

**Cadence Ramp** · Medium · Medium
- **Test:** format held constant, posting tripled to 3+ a day for 2 weeks (batch-film a week in one sitting).
- **Win =** one breakout post (5× median views) and a rising 7-day view trend.
- **Then:** win → hold the cadence. Flat → volume isn't the lever; back to hooks and formats.
- *Rummer (Uray, reports $300K in 2024): 200M+ organic views from volume.*

## 2. Long-form content

**Teach-the-Pain Tutorial** · Medium · Medium
- **Test:** one focused how-to answering the question customers ask most, titled for that exact search, no long intro, the product shown as the "how".
- **Win =** 3+ paying customers (or [N] qualified signups) within 90 days, even at a few hundred views.
- **Then:** win → a weekly series, one search each. Views but no signups → the path to the product is the gap, not the topic.
- *Bulk Mockup (Vikash, reports $12K/mo): a 370-view video drove $345 MRR from 3 customers.*

**Long-Form Story Post** · Low · Medium · ⭐
- **Test:** one long post (thread, essay, newsletter) telling a specific story with a number in it — a before/after, a milestone, a mistake — ending in a soft mention and link; reply to every comment.
- **Win =** [N] signups or a clear click spike within a week.
- **Then:** win → lock a weekly cadence. Flat → a more concrete, specific result.
- *CodeGuide.dev (Sajjad, reports $42K/mo): ~4 threads and 3 long posts a week on X.*

**Creator Collab** · High · High
- **Test:** one long-form video with (or a genuine segment from) a creator whose audience is the customer, with a unique link or code.
- **Win =** [N] signups at a cost per customer under payback within 30 days.
- **Then:** win → more collabs like it. Flat → wrong creator, not wrong format.
- *boot.dev (Lane Wagner, reports ~$1M/mo): YouTube collabs scaled it past 25,000 paying members.*

## 3. Communities

**Framing Test** · Low · Medium · ⭐
- **Test:** the same demo in two comparable communities — one framed as "I built this because…", one as a feature announcement.
- **Win =** the story framing gets 2× the tagged clicks and stays up, within a week.
- **Then:** win → the template everywhere. Both removed → you're posting like an ad; go value-first.
- *Save Wise (Ovnish, reports $25K/mo): matched the group's vocabulary before posting.*

**Value-First Thread** · Medium · High
- **Test:** the definitive answer to the community's most-repeated question, with the product as one step in the "how", no pitch.
- **Win =** 50+ trials or 20+ paying from one community within 2 weeks.
- **Then:** win → a repeatable series. Flat → the buyers may be elsewhere; test a new community.
- *Note Forms (Julien Nahum, reports $37K/mo): helpful posts in Notion communities; the first wave went viral.*

**New-Community Expansion** · Medium · Medium
- **Test:** the winning post in one new named community a day for 2 weeks, each with its own tag.
- **Win =** one community converting 2× the current best.
- **Then:** win → double down on the top 1–2, drop the rest. None convert → framing or product, not reach.
- *App Alchemy (Diego, reports $17K MRR): organic posts across many subreddits, no ad spend.*

## 4. Search

**Free-Tool Wedge** · High · High
- **Test:** one ungated single-purpose tool (calculator, converter, generator, template) for a high-intent query, with a soft path into the paid product.
- **Win =** top-3 for its query and 15%+ of visitors signing up within 3 months.
- **Then:** win → the next free tool. Ranks but no signups → fix the path to paid, not the tool.
- *Super Demo (Joseph, reports $250K/mo): free tools drive ~20% of traffic.*

**Comparison Page** · Medium · High
- **Test:** one honest "[competitor] alternative" or "best [job] tool for [customer]" page that includes the product.
- **Win =** page 1 and 10+ trials a month within 3 months.
- **Then:** win → the next comparison. No rank → a lower-competition query.
- *BankStatementConverter.com (Angus Cheng, reports $40K MRR): ~40K visits a month of pure intent.*

**AI-Answer Pass** · Medium · Medium
- **Test:** ask ChatGPT, Perplexity and Gemini the category question; note who's named and which pages are cited; publish the comparison content they draw on and get mentioned on the third-party pages they cite; re-ask weekly.
- **Win =** the product named for the core category query within 6 weeks.
- **Then:** win → adjacent queries. Absent → work the cited third-party sites.

## 5. Native channels and platform ecosystems

On a store, marketplace or registry the levers are the listing and its ranking signals, not posting: listing conversion (name, first line, images, price shown — use the store's own listing tests where it has them), ranking for the customer's search terms, review volume and recency, and installs that stay installed. Measure in the store's own console.

**Listing Rewrite** · Low · High · ⭐
- **Test:** the top keyword in the name, a first line that names the job in the customer's words, the first image showing the magic moment.
- **Win =** 20%+ lift in listing-to-install (or a keyword ranking gain) against the previous 3 weeks.
- **Then:** win → the next keyword cluster. Flat → ranking, not the listing, is the constraint.
- *Migo (John MacAvoy, reports $30K/mo): location keywords and localised listings to 5M+ downloads.*

**Review-Velocity Push** · Medium · Medium
- **Test:** a review prompt right after the first successful output, plus a personal ask to every happy early user, over 2 weeks.
- **Win =** 20+ reviews averaging 4.7+ and a measurable ranking bump within 2 weeks.
- **Then:** win → keep the prompt permanently. No movement → reviews aren't the lever here.
- *Cut Coach (Ethan, reports $20K/mo): a high rating and community sharing during wrestling season.*

**Integration Wedge** · High · High
- **Test:** the smallest useful connector to the one platform the customer uses daily, listed in its directory.
- **Win =** 25+ signups a month from the platform within 6 weeks of listing.
- **Then:** win → the next integration. Flat → wrong platform or weak listing.
- *Screenshot One (Dmytro, reports $12K/mo): Zapier and Make integrations.*

**Adjacent Listing** · Low · Medium · ⭐
- **Test:** the same product listed in one more directory or store the shape's buyers browse (another browser's store, a second agent directory, a second marketplace for the format).
- **Win =** [N] installs or sales a month from the new listing within 4 weeks.
- **Then:** win → the next adjacent place. Flat → keep the listing, stop investing in it.

## 6. Launch platforms

**Lifetime-Deal Probe** · Medium · High
- **Test:** a 3-day lifetime deal (a deal site or your own) with three tiers, the middle one the obvious pick; onboard buyers personally.
- **Win =** $10K+ in the window, with 20+ buyers active weekly afterwards.
- **Then:** win → keep the cohort as reviewers and referrers. Miss → the offer or audience is off.
- *Supergrow (Devin, reports $230K/yr): $79/$199/$299 tiers made $65K in 3 days.*

**Product Hunt / Show HN Launch** · Medium · Medium
- **Test:** the platform that fits the buyer; demo video, copy and first comment ready; the small audience rallied for the first hour.
- **Win =** top-5 of the day or the front page, 200+ signups and lasting backlinks.
- **Then:** win → reuse the asset kit at the next milestone. Flop → an empty room; build the audience first.
- *ChartDB (Jonathan, reports $9K MRR): a Hacker News launch and 21K+ GitHub stars.*

**Milestone Re-Launch** · Low · Medium · ⭐
- **Test:** recent work bundled into one notable feature, teased, then launched to the list and communities.
- **Win =** beats the last launch's signups or revenue.
- **Then:** win → a monthly rhythm. Flat → space them out.
- *Creator Buddy (Alex Finn): months of building in public, then $100K ARR on launch day.*

## 7. Outreach

**First-Line Test** · Low · High · ⭐
- **Test:** the same offer to 100 prospects — 50 with a first line about something real about them, 50 generic.
- **Win =** 2× the reply rate within a week.
- **Then:** win → personalise every send. Flat → the offer is weak, not the opener.
- *Birdhouse (Marcos, reports $65K/mo): disciplined personal DMs on X.*

**Offer Test** · Low · High · ⭐
- **Test:** 3 one-line offers, each a different outcome, to 30–50 matched prospects each.
- **Win =** one books 2× the calls or replies within 2 weeks.
- **Then:** win → the standard pitch. None → revisit who you're targeting.
- *Once (Brian Shin, reports $20K/mo in 83 days): a tight commitment ask to 300 people.*

**Follow-Up Sequence** · Low · Medium · ⭐
- **Test:** three short follow-ups (a bump, something useful, a close-the-loop) on days 2, 4 and 7 to everyone who didn't reply.
- **Win =** 50%+ more replies from the same list.
- **Then:** win → follow-ups in every sequence. Flat → the list or offer.
- *ListKit (Andre, reports $200K MRR): cold email with disciplined sequences.*

## 8. Influencer / creator

**Micro-Creator Batch** · Medium · High
- **Test:** 5–10 micro-creators whose audience is the customer, a small flat fee (~$100–150) plus free access, a code each.
- **Win =** at least one creator's cost per install under payback.
- **Then:** win → re-run and find lookalikes. None → audience mismatch.
- *Social Wizard (Kelechi, reports $60K/mo): ~$120 a video to micro-streamers.*

**Brief/Angle Test** · Medium · Medium
- **Test:** 2–3 angles in the customer's words spread across comparable creators.
- **Win =** one angle converts 2× the others.
- **Then:** win → in every brief. Flat → let the best creators freestyle.
- *Cal AI (Blake Anderson, reports $1M+/mo): creators showing the magic moment.*

**Boost the Winner** · Medium · High
- **Test:** the best-converting creator video, with usage rights, run as a paid ad to a matched audience.
- **Win =** cost per install stays under payback at 3× the organic spend.
- **Then:** holds → scale spend in steps. Breaks → back to seeding.

## 9. Partnerships

**Audience-Borrow Drop** · Medium · High
- **Test:** one newsletter swap, webinar or bundle with a partner whose audience is the customer, offering them something first.
- **Win =** 50+ signups or 10+ paying within a week.
- **Then:** win → three more partners like it. Flat → audience mismatch or a weak offer to the partner.
- *LaunchFast (Hassam, reports $21.8K MRR in 90 days): a coaching programme's audience of Amazon sellers.*

**Affiliate-Partner Recruitment** · Medium · Medium
- **Test:** 3–5 operators the audience trusts, a clear recurring commission, a link and assets each.
- **Win =** 10+ paying customers within 60 days.
- **Then:** win → recruit more like the top partner. Flat → commission or fit.
- *Lancer (Ivan, reports $10K/mo in 3–4 months): connector and affiliate partnerships, no ads.*

## 10. Email and lifecycle

**Welcome Series** · Medium · High
- **Test:** three emails on signup (day 0, 1, 3) — the quick win, the next step, the proof or upgrade — each linking to one action toward the magic moment.
- **Win =** signups in the series reach the magic moment clearly more often than those without it, within 30 days.
- **Then:** win → the default, add a fourth. Flat → the friction is in the product, not the inbox (run the product audit).

**Win-Back Sequence** · Low · Medium · ⭐
- **Test:** 2–3 emails to anyone inactive for [14–30] days: what they left unfinished, a reason to return, a last nudge.
- **Win =** 5–10% of the dormant group reactivated within 30 days, against a group that got nothing.
- **Then:** win → permanent; test offers. Flat → the churn is about value; fix the product.

**Newsletter Broadcast** · Medium · Medium
- **Test:** a sustainable weekly or fortnightly send, one useful angle, one link back to the product.
- **Win =** open and click rates hold across 4 sends and each send brings a visible bump in return visits.
- **Then:** win → lock the cadence, grow the list. Decays → sharpen the value or send less.
- *Marketing Examined (Alex Garcia, reports ~$800K/yr): a useful broadcast grown from ~2K to 8–9K readers.*

## 11. Referrals and affiliates

**Built-In Loop** · Medium · High
- **Test:** a light "made with [product]" mark and link on whatever users already share.
- **Win =** referral visits reach 15%+ of new signups within 60 days.
- **Then:** win → strengthen it (prominence, incentive). Flat → users aren't sharing; fix retention first.
- *Stagetimer (Lucas Herman, reports $25K/mo): a "powered by" logo drove ~a third of the business.*

**Referral Incentive Test** · Medium · Medium
- **Test:** a give-get invite offer shown right after a win.
- **Win =** 10%+ of active users send an invite within 30 days.
- **Then:** win → tune the reward. Silent → the product isn't recommendable yet; don't force it.

## 12. Ads

**Offer Validation** · Low · High · ⭐
- **Test:** 4–6 plain text-on-image ads, each stating a different version of the offer, to one tight audience at a small daily budget (~$50–100 over 5–7 days), all to the landing page or listing.
- **Win =** one ad's click-through is ~2× the rest, with real clicks behind it.
- **Then:** win → that line becomes the headline everywhere (page, listing, hooks). Nothing clicks → the offer isn't landing; re-run the offer step before spending more.
- *Make Logo AI (Nico, reports ~$85K in 6 months): static ads revealed which message to fund.*

**Creative Volume Test** · Medium · High
- **Test:** the best organic post cut into 5–10 variations (hooks, thumbnails) for one audience; losers killed fast.
- **Win =** one creative holds cost per customer under payback within 2 weeks.
- **Then:** win → scale it and make more like it. None → the hook didn't transfer; don't widen spend.

**Landing-Page Match** · Medium · High
- **Test:** one ad's traffic split between a page that mirrors its promise and the homepage.
- **Win =** 2× ad-to-signup on the matched page within 2 weeks.
- **Then:** win → a page per ad angle. Flat → the offer is the gap.

## From the product audit

When `docs/audit/REPORT.md` exists, its conversion and retention findings are ready-made experiments: each finding's recommendation is the test, the funnel stage it sits on is the metric. Typical pairs: a weak first screen → Listing Rewrite or Landing-Page Match; a long path to the magic moment → Welcome Series plus the in-product fix; users who don't come back → Win-Back Sequence. A Critical finding that needs a code change belongs in the build plan, not here — say so.

## Experiment failure patterns

- **The Vanity Experiment.** The win is views or followers. Redesign until it's revenue-shaped.
- **Too Many in Flight.** More than three, or more than one channel.
- **The Thresholdless Test.** No number and no date — an activity, not an experiment.
- **The Generic Experiment.** "Post more", "improve SEO". Rewrite until only this product could run it.
- **The Big-Bet-First Trap.** A high-effort experiment while quick wins sit untouched.
- **The Unlogged Result.** Results never written in the tracker, so nothing compounds.
- **Optimising a Dead Channel.** Experiments poured into a channel that isn't reaching buyers. Back to Strategy.

## Scaling a winner

Only rows the tracker shows as **Win** and **double down** get scaled. For each, three moves:

- **Do more** — the volume lever with a target, held only while the result holds: "1 post a day → 3", "$50/day → $200/day at the same cost per customer", "50 sends a week → 200".
- **Do better** — the next experiment on the same channel that lifts the win (it goes to the queue). Doing better never stops, even once doing more is automated.
- **Automate** — the manual steps handed to tools, with one step kept human.

**The maturity ladder** — name where each process sits and the next rung. Move one rung at a time, one process at a time:

Manual (the member does every step) → Templated (a checklist or template) → Assisted (tools draft, the member approves) → Automated (runs on a trigger or schedule) → Delegated (a contractor runs the documented process).

**What to keep human, by channel:**

| Channel | Automate the plumbing | Keep human |
|---|---|---|
| Short-form | Clipping, captions, scheduling, pulling results | The hook and the member on camera |
| Long-form | Drafting outlines, repurposing into clips and posts, the calendar | The story and the opinion |
| Communities | Listening for keyword mentions, drafting replies to review | Posting and replying as a real member |
| Search | Keyword and page research, drafts, rank tracking | The angle, the honest comparison, the final edit |
| Native channel | The review prompt, scheduled console reports, release notes on every version | Replying to reviews |
| Outreach | List building, enrichment, research per prospect, sequencing, follow-ups | The first line and every reply |
| Creators | Finding and shortlisting creators, tracking codes | The brief and the relationship |
| Email | Triggers, segments, scheduling | The welcome email's voice and the broadcast's point |

**Scaling failure patterns:**

- **Scaling the Unproven.** More time or money into something the tracker never proved.
- **Automating the Magic Away.** Automating the part that works *because* it's human — the member's voice, a real community reply, a personal first line.
- **Premature Scale.** More volume or spend before the numbers hold at the higher level.
- **More Without Better.** Scaling a mediocre win instead of sharpening it.
- **The Tool Hoard.** Ten tools instead of automating the one step that costs the most time.
- **No Guardrail Metric.** Throughput up, conversion quietly down. Every automation watches one quality number and has a stop rule.
- **Automating Out of the Loop.** Removing the member so fully they lose sight of real customers.
