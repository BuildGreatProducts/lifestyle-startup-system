# Channel playbooks

How each channel type works for a first product, what "working" looks like, and why each one fails. Use it to pick and rank the three channels in Strategy and to write each channel's "Working =" line. The named places for *this* niche come from live research ([channel-research.md](channel-research.md)), never from this file.

Founder figures are self-reported in Starter Story interviews, paraphrased, compiled June 2026. Quote them as "reports", not facts.

## The rule

The customer already chose the channel; the member's job is to find where they gather and show up there. If the member can't name the exact subreddit, search query, group or store where their first 100 customers already are, the plan isn't ready. **One channel at a time:** start Channel 1, and start the next only when the current one hits its "Working =" number. Seven channels at 10% effort all fail quietly; one at 100% produces a number you can read.

## Picking the starting channel

First, the shape: if it has a native store, marketplace, registry or directory ([../../product-offer/references/shapes.md](../../product-offer/references/shapes.md)), that's one of the three by default (see [The native channel](#the-native-channel)). Then stop at the first yes:

1. **Can the result be shown in under 60 seconds** — a before/after, a satisfying output, a relatable problem? → **Short-form content**, paired with communities.
2. **Does the customer gather in a nameable place** — a subreddit, Discord, Facebook group, forum, association? → **Communities.**
3. **Do people search for the problem** ("how do I…", "best X for Y", a converter or calculator query)? → **Search.**
4. **Will the member teach and play a longer game** — tutorials, a newsletter, a podcast? → **Long-form content.**
5. **Does it extend a platform the customer already lives in** (Notion, Shopify, Chrome, WordPress, an app store)? → **Platform ecosystems.**
6. **Is it over ~$100/month or sold to businesses, with a findable list of buyers?** → **Outreach.**
7. **Do creators the customer follows exist, and would a 60-second demo from them land?** → **Influencer / creator.**
8. **Is there already a list** — newsletter, waitlist, past clients? → **Email**, to convert it.
9. **None of these, or stuck?** → Go back to the customer research: read where they complain (subreddit and forum threads about the pain) until a place can be named.

Launch platforms, partnerships, referrals and ads amplify a channel that already works; they're rarely the starting channel.

## The fit matrix

| Channel | Best for | Price it supports | First signal | Ongoing cost | Difficulty |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Short-form content | B2C, prosumer, visual | Any (strong at $5–30/mo) | Days–weeks | Time | Medium |
| Long-form content | Prosumer, B2B, authority | Any | Weeks–months | Time, low | Medium–high |
| Communities | B2C, prosumer, niche B2B | Any | Days | Time | Low–medium |
| Search (SEO and AI answers) | Everyone, esp. prosumer/B2B | Any | 3–6 months | Low | Medium–high |
| Platform ecosystems | Prosumer, B2B | Any | Weeks | Platform fee | Medium |
| Launch platforms | Everyone, once | Any (strong for lifetime deals) | One day | Low–platform fee | Low |
| Outreach | B2B, high-ticket, services | $100/mo+ | Days | Low (tools) | Medium |
| Influencer / creator | B2C | $5–30/mo | Weeks | Per post | Medium |
| Partnerships | Prosumer, B2B | Any | Weeks–months | Revenue share | Medium–high |
| Email and lifecycle | Anyone with a list | Any | Days | Low | Low–medium |
| Referrals and affiliates | Anyone with happy users | Any | Months | Revenue share | Medium |
| Ads | Anyone whose funnel already converts | Lifetime value must cover cost per customer | Days | Ongoing spend | High |

First revenue almost always comes from the organic channels (the first five). The price band is a hard check: a $9/month app can't fund cold outreach; a $5,000/month service shouldn't lead with TikTok.

## The twelve channels

Each: when it fits · how to start · **Working =** (a first-30-to-90-day bar to adapt) · why it fails · a real example.

### 1. Short-form content (TikTok, Reels, Shorts)
- **Fits:** B2C and prosumer, $5–30/month, a problem you can show in one clip.
- **Start:** search the niche hashtag, sort by most-liked, copy the top video's *format* (hook, beats, call to action) with your own demo. First 2–3 seconds lead with the customer's pain, not the product. One a day for two weeks before judging. Link to the feature, never the homepage.
- **Working =** one post past ~100K organic views and 50+ trial signups inside two weeks of daily posting.
- **Fails when:** posting once and quitting; counting views that never reach a buy action.
- *PuffCount (Steven Cravada, reports $40K/mo):* rebuilt a 3.7M-like "vape-in-water" format from #quitvaping around his app; 50M+ organic views before any ad spend.

### 2. Long-form content (YouTube tutorials, newsletters, podcasts, long posts)
- **Fits:** prosumer and B2B, buyers who research before they buy, a member willing to teach.
- **Start:** answer the question customers ask most — one search phrase per video, no long intro — or a weekly written story with a number in it, or a guest spot on a podcast the customer already hears.
- **Working =** one asset converting steadily for months, e.g. 3+ paying customers in 90 days from a few hundred views.
- **Fails when:** expecting short-form speed; rambling — each asset answers one thing, fast.
- *Bulk Mockup (Vikash, reports $12K/mo):* a 370-view how-to video drove $345 MRR from 3 customers. *AudioPen (Louis Pereira, reports $15K/mo):* built the audience building in public on X until strangers asked to pay.

### 3. Communities (subreddits, Discords, Facebook groups, forums, associations)
- **Fits:** a nameable group, ideally under ~100K members, around the exact problem. Any price.
- **Start:** map 10–20 places; lurk a week and copy down the words they use for the pain; answer questions and share the workaround before ever linking; when you post, "I built this because X kept eating my evenings", not an ad. Keyword alerts (e.g. F5Bot) catch the moment someone describes the problem.
- **Working =** 20+ paying users from one community inside 30 days, with members referring each other unprompted.
- **Fails when:** a link on day one and a ban; mistaking one delighted commenter for a market.
- *Save Wise (Ovnish, reports $25K/mo):* lurked in points-stacking Facebook groups for weeks, used their vocabulary, zero ad spend. *Note Forms (Julien Nahum, reports $37K/mo):* seeded Notion subreddits and groups; the first wave went viral.

### 4. Search (Google and AI answers)
- **Fits:** people search with intent — "best X for Y", "[competitor] alternative", a converter or calculator. Any price.
- **Start:** the few queries closest to a purchase, not the big vanity ones. For a first product the strongest move is a **free single-purpose tool** with no signup gate that ranks and hands users to the paid product. For AI answers: ask ChatGPT, Perplexity and Gemini the category question, note who they name and which pages they cite, and write the comparison content they draw on.
- **Working =** one page or free tool top-3 for its query inside ~3 months, converting 15–20% of visitors to signup; the product named in AI answers for the core query.
- **Fails when:** pivoting away in week 3 — search pays in month 3+; ranking for traffic that never buys.
- *BankStatementConverter.com (Angus Cheng, reports $40K MRR):* ranks for exactly what accountants search; ~40K visits a month of pure intent. *Super Demo (Joseph, reports $250K/mo):* dozens of free ungated tools drive ~20% of traffic.

### 5. Platform ecosystems (marketplaces of a platform the customer lives in)
- **Fits:** prosumer and B2B tools that extend a host product or fit an app-store keyword cluster.
- **Start:** one platform, the narrowest useful extension, a listing written for how users there search; seed the first reviews from the platform's own communities.
- **Working =** the listing ranks for its core in-store keyword and brings installs without daily posting.
- **Fails when:** treating the platform as owned — a policy change can cut supply overnight. Capture an email at first use.
- *Data Fetcher (Andy Cloke, reports $23K MRR):* an Airtable extension listed in Airtable's marketplace, no audience of his own. *Barn2 (Katie Keith, reports ~$150K/mo):* 19 WordPress plugins grown on ecosystem search and feature requests.

### 6. Launch platforms (Product Hunt, Hacker News, AppSumo-style deal sites)
- **Fits:** everyone, once — a moment, not a channel. Strong for lifetime-deal-friendly tools and founders with no audience.
- **Start:** match the platform to the buyer (Product Hunt: makers; Show HN: developers; deal sites: SaaS bargain hunters). Assets ready in advance (demo video, first comment, a real price); rally the small audience you have for the first hour. For a lifetime deal, three tiers with the middle as the obvious pick.
- **Working =** $10K+ in a deal window with 20–25 buyers active weekly afterwards, or a launch that nets backlinks, reviews and a few hundred people to re-contact.
- **Fails when:** the launch *is* the plan — the spike fades in 48 hours; launching to an empty room.
- *Supergrow (Devin, reports $230K/yr):* a 3-day RocketHub deal at $79/$199/$299 made $65K and kept ~250 evangelists. *Castmagic (Blaine):* an AppSumo launch brought ~10,000 users and ~$350K.

### 7. Outreach (cold and warm DMs, email, one-to-one selling)
- **Fits:** B2B, high-ticket, productized services — a buyer worth a personal message, and a list you can build.
- **Start:** exact buyer (role, company type, trigger event), a small high-quality list, a first line about something real about *them*. Ask for a call or a confirmed date, not a sale. Small batches; fix the message before adding volume.
- **Working =** the first handful of paying customers (or 10+ firm commitments) traceable to outreach within a few weeks.
- **Fails when:** one template blasted to thousands — it burns the sending domain and teaches nothing.
- *Once (Brian Shin, reports $20K/mo in 83 days):* DM'd ~300 strangers through wedding hashtags and required 10 confirmed event dates before writing code.

### 8. Influencer / creator
- **Fits:** B2C at $5–30/month with a visual hook and creators who serve exactly that audience.
- **Start:** micro-creators, not big names (often ~$100–150 a video), briefed on the problem, keeping their own voice; many small placements, each with its own code or link.
- **Working =** a repeatable cost per install under payback, with at least one creator-and-hook pair you can re-run.
- **Fails when:** one big vanity placement with no tracking; a large audience that isn't the customer.
- *Social Wizard (Kelechi, reports $60K/mo):* paid micro-streamers ~$120 a video to demo to 16–24 year-olds.

### 9. Partnerships (integrations, co-marketing, bundles)
- **Fits:** prosumer and B2B with a natural complement whose audience is the customer.
- **Start:** list the products and audience-owners the customer trusts; build the integration so you appear in *their* directory, or offer the partner something first (revenue share, a co-built asset). One warm partner before three.
- **Working =** one partner sending a steady, attributable stream of signups.
- **Fails when:** a "let's cross-promote" handshake with no owner, date or tracking.
- *LaunchFast (Hassam, reports $21.8K MRR in 90 days):* partnered with a coaching programme for Amazon sellers. *Screenshot One (Dmytro, reports $12K/mo):* Zapier and Make integrations.

### 10. Email and lifecycle
- **Fits:** anyone capturing signups. The one channel the member owns; it converts the traffic every other channel sends.
- **Start:** capture emails from day one. First a **welcome series** that walks a signup to the magic moment, then a **win-back** for people who go quiet, then a regular broadcast worth opening. Every email links to one action.
- **Working =** a welcome series that measurably lifts activation, and email driving a steady share of conversions and reactivations.
- **Fails when:** one broadcast blasted to everyone instead of triggered sequences; a list that's never mailed and decays.
- *Morning Brew:* a daily newsletter plus a reader referral programme — the list was the business.

### 11. Referrals and affiliates
- **Fits:** after there are happy users; strongest when usage is visible to non-users or the niche is tight-knit.
- **Start:** earn it first — referrals compound only on retention. Then a light "made with" mark on anything users share, an easy invite right after a win, or a recurring commission for creators whose audience is the customer (check the margin funds it).
- **Working =** a climbing share of new signups from existing users or affiliates, with no paid spend.
- **Fails when:** bolting an incentive onto a product nobody loves yet.
- *Stagetimer (Lucas Herman, reports $25K/mo):* a small "powered by" logo on shared timers brought roughly a third of the business.

### 12. Ads
- **Fits:** only after an organic channel has proven the hook and the numbers say a customer is worth more than they cost to win.
- **Start:** promote the organic post that already worked, to one tight audience, into a page matched to the ad (not the homepage). Kill losers fast; scale only creative that pays back.
- **Working =** spend $X, reliably get back more than $X within the payback window.
- **Fails when:** ads are used to fix a funnel that doesn't convert for free; creative fatigue — winners decay in weeks.
- *PuffCount:* switched on TikTok and Meta ads only after organic proved the hook. *Make Logo AI (Nico, reports ~$85K in 6 months):* static ads into audience-specific landing pages.

## The native channel

The shape's store, marketplace, registry or directory is where buyers of that shape already go looking. The places for each shape are in [../../product-offer/references/shapes.md](../../product-offer/references/shapes.md); what ranks there today comes from [channel-research.md](channel-research.md).

- **One of the three is the native channel**, unless there's a stated reason (the store bans the category, the buyers are a named list of companies, the member already owns a stronger channel). Say the reason in that slot's "Why" line.
- **It's rarely the first.** Native discovery rewards products that already have installs and reviews, so it's often Channel 2: Channel 1 sends the first users, whose reviews lift the ranking.
- **The listing is the landing page.** Name, first line, images and reviews do the job a landing page does on the web.
- **Build the owned list anyway.** Capture an email or account at first use.

Discovery on almost any store or directory comes down to five forces. Work them in this order:

1. **Relevance** — the name, first line, keywords and categories use the words customers type (from the customer section of the offer), not category jargon.
2. **Conversion** — of those who see the listing, how many install or buy. Images, the first line and the price shown do most of it.
3. **Social proof** — number, recency and average of reviews. Most stores weight recent ones, so a steady trickle beats a launch-day burst.
4. **Usage and retention** — installs that stay installed, servers that keep getting called, no refunds.
5. **Curation** — features, badges, staff picks, official directories. Earned by quality and policy compliance, usually requested through a form, never bought.

Plain lists and registries with no ranking (a GitHub "awesome" list, a metadata registry) reward the first two plus being listed at all.

**First moves by shape** (adapt; confirm the places in shapes.md):

| Shape | First move on the native channel |
|---|---|
| Web app | One page per high-intent search ("[job] tool for [customer]", "[competitor] alternative"); a listing in the integration directory of the tool the customer already uses |
| Mobile app | Keyword research for the customer's search terms, a listing built around the magic moment, and a review prompt right after it |
| Desktop app | An "alternative to [incumbent]" listing in an alternatives directory and a comparison page; most desktop apps sell direct and use the stores for trust |
| Browser extension | A listing around the one job, five screenshots of that job, a review prompt after the first successful use |
| Agent plugin or skill | A public repo whose README shows the first output in under a minute, submission to each official directory, a before/after demo in the customer's own community (not only AI-builder circles). For a skill, the description is also the in-agent ranking signal |
| MCP server | Publish to the official registry, submit to the directories of the two clients the customer uses most, write the "[system] MCP server" page that system's users will search for |
| Chat assistant | A store listing naming one job, and a "try it" link used live where the customer already talks |
| Developer tool | A README with a copy-paste quickstart to the first successful call in under five minutes, registry keywords in developers' own terms, a Show HN once a real user has succeeded |
| Productized service | No store: a named list of 30 buyers and a fixed-price offer sent personally; a freelance-marketplace or partner-directory listing second, priced to win the first reviews |
| Website (directory, newsletter, community) | The twenty pages answering the twenty questions the customer searches; a recommendation swap with two newsletters the same reader already gets |
| Digital product | A listing on the one marketplace where buyers of that format search, with preview images of the finished result; every buyer onto an email list for the next product |

## Adding a second channel

Only after the first hits its "Working =" number with revenue you can repeat. Pick one that shares an asset with the first:

| First channel | Natural next | Why they compound |
|---|---|---|
| Short-form | Creators, then ads | The same videos fuel all three |
| Long-form | Search, then referrals | Tutorials rank and get passed around |
| Communities | A launch, then referrals | An engaged community powers launch day and word of mouth |
| Email | Referrals, then ads | The list powers invites and warm ad audiences |
| Search | Platform ecosystems | Both catch the moment of intent |
| Outreach | Partnerships | One-to-one, then one-to-many relationships |
| Platform ecosystems | A launch, then search | Launch reviews lift store and search ranking |

## Starter sequences

| Selling… | Start | Then | Amplify |
|---|---|---|---|
| A consumer app ($5–30/mo, visual) | Short-form | Communities | Creators, then ads |
| A prosumer tool on a platform | Platform ecosystem | Communities | A launch |
| A niche hobby or community product | Communities | Short-form | Referrals |
| A B2B tool or high-ticket product | Outreach | Search (comparison pages) | Partnerships |
| A productized service | Outreach to a named list | A marketplace or partner-directory listing | Referrals from delivered clients |
| A content- or audience-first product | Long-form | Email | Referrals and affiliates |

## Failure patterns

Name them out loud when you see them:

- **The Seven-Channel Spread.** A little of everything. Force three, ranked, one at a time.
- **The Preference Channel.** The channel the member enjoys (usually a blog or building in public) over the one the customer is on. A wrestler's app belongs in wrestling communities even if its maker would rather podcast.
- **The Unnamed Channel.** "Communities", "social", "SEO" are categories. A channel is a place the member can show up in tomorrow morning: r/X, the Y Discord, the query "Z".
- **The Generic Plan.** "Post on Reddit", "do some SEO". If an action would read the same for another product, rewrite it.
- **The Premature-Ads Reflex.** Ads or a referral programme before an organic channel has proven the hook. Paid is never Channel 1 for a pre-revenue product.
- **The Vanity Goal.** "Working = 100K views." Every bar is a payment, a paying user, a booked call, an activated user — with a date.
- **The Skipped Store.** A shape with a native store and no plan to rank there.
- **The Competitor Copy.** Lifting a competitor's channel without checking it fits this customer and price. PuffCount's TikTok works because quitting vaping is visual and emotional; a B2B API isn't.
- **Quitting Before It Compounds.** Search, communities and content pay in month 3, not week 3. Check the cadence was actually held before calling a channel dead.
- **Abandoning a Working Channel.** If Reddit is paying rent, run it until it genuinely caps out before chasing something that sounds bigger.
