# Channel research

How to turn channel *types* into the named places, people and searches for this member's niche, and how to see how competitors actually get customers. Run it fresh every time: communities grow, die and change their rules; stores change fees and ranking; creators move on. The value of the whole plan is specificity, and specificity comes from this research, not from the member's gut or a stored list.

## Start from what the docs already say

`docs/2-OFFER.md` → Customer names where they hang out, and Offer → proof and Pricing → anchors name competitors. Start there and widen. Anything that already pulled — a reply, a signup, a sale, a past client — beats every search result: ask where it came from, and treat the people behind it as a channel.

## 1. Map the native channel

From the shape's Native channels column and Go to market note in [../../product-offer/references/shapes.md](../../product-offer/references/shapes.md), open each native place and search it the way the customer would:

| Look for | How | Bring back |
|---|---|---|
| What ranks today | Search the store or directory for the customer's 3–5 likeliest terms (their words, from the offer) | The top 5 listings per term: name, first line, review count and average, price shown, last update |
| The bar to clear | Open the top 3 listings | What their first image and first line promise; what reviewers praise and complain about (complaints are positioning) |
| Categories and curated lists | Browse the category pages, staff picks, "awesome" lists, official directories | Which lists the leaders sit in, and how to get in (submission form, review, criteria) |
| Current rules and fees | The platform's own developer or seller documentation, dated | Listing fees, revenue share, review rules, what's banned. Never quote a fee from memory |

## 2. Name the places

For each candidate channel, turn the category into instances someone could show up in tomorrow. Names, not categories: "r/[niche] — 200K members, 30+ posts a day, self-promotion only in the Friday thread" beats "accounting communities".

| Channel | Search for | Check before you list it |
|---|---|---|
| Communities | `site:reddit.com [pain in the customer's words]`; "[profession] Discord", "[niche] Facebook group", "[craft] forum"; associations, Slack groups and member communities for the profession; the communities competitors' founders post in | Size *and* activity (posts per day, replies per post, last post date); the self-promotion rules; whether the pain actually comes up (find 2–3 threads) |
| Search | The customer's questions typed into Google and an AI assistant; autocomplete and "People also ask"; "[competitor] alternative", "best [job] tool for [customer]", "[task] template/calculator/converter" | Who ranks now (big publishers or small sites — small sites mean it's winnable); whether the intent is to buy, learn or browse; which pages AI answers cite |
| Short-form | The niche hashtags on TikTok, Instagram and YouTube Shorts, sorted by most-liked; the customer's pain as a search | The top 3 videos' hook, length and call to action; posting volume of the accounts that win |
| Long-form | YouTube searches for the customer's how-to questions; podcasts and newsletters in the niche (podcast apps, newsletter directories, "best [niche] newsletter") | Audience size signals (views, subscribers, reviews); whether they take guests, sponsors or swaps |
| Creators | Who the customer follows: ask the member, then search the niche hashtags and the "people also follow" of known accounts | Audience match (comments from the customer, not peers); engagement over follower count; whether they've done paid demos before |
| Platform ecosystems | The marketplaces of the tools the customer uses daily (from the offer); integration directories of those tools | The top listings for the job (as in section 1); whether a small integration could be listed |
| Outreach | Where a named list of buyers comes from: LinkedIn searches by role and company type, association member lists, directories of businesses in the niche, trigger events (new hires, funding, launches, seasonal deadlines) | That 30+ real, reachable buyers exist; one trigger that makes now the moment |
| Partnerships | Products and audience-owners the customer already trusts: complementary tools, coaches and courses, newsletters, agencies | What they'd get from a deal; whether they already partner with anyone |
| Launch platforms | Recent launches of comparable products on Product Hunt, Show HN and deal sites | How they placed, what they priced, what the comments asked |

## 3. Tear down competitors' distribution

Take 3–5 competitors from the offer's proof and price anchors (or find them) and reverse-engineer how they actually get customers. How the proven money in the category distributes is the strongest single signal of what will work — and of what's saturated.

- **Search footprint:** which queries they rank for and which pages bring the traffic (search their brand plus the category; look at their blog, free tools and comparison pages).
- **Store presence:** their listing, its review count and velocity, the categories it sits in.
- **Launch history:** Product Hunt, Show HN, deal-site listings, and how each went.
- **Social and creators:** where they post, their best-performing posts, the creators who've featured them.
- **Communities:** where the founder or team answers questions; where customers recommend them unprompted.
- **Founder interviews:** search "[competitor] founder interview", "how [competitor] got first customers", Indie Hackers and Starter Story posts — founders often say exactly which channel worked.

Use it for signal, not mimicry: a channel that carries a viral consumer app won't carry a $99/month B2B tool.

## 4. For experiments: what's working on this channel now

Once a focus channel is set, look at the last 60–90 days only — tactics decay fast:

- The hooks, formats, angles, offers and posting cadence getting traction in this niche on this channel right now.
- What 3–5 competitors are currently doing there: their best recent posts, their current listing copy and images, their current offers and landing pages.
- For a native channel: listing changes the leaders made recently (new images, new first line, a price change) and how their reviews moved.

## What to bring back

- **For Strategy:** 10–20 named places across the candidate channels, each with a size or activity signal, its self-promotion rule where it has one, and a link; the native channel's top listings and the bar they set; 3–5 competitors' channels in one line each.
- **For Experiments:** 3–5 concrete, current examples (a hook, a listing change, an offer) to tailor into experiments.
- Show the member the named places and ask which look right and where they're already active. Their confirmation goes into the doc; the full trail stays in the conversation.

## Trust and citing

- Open the page; a search snippet is not a source. Subscriber counts on a sidebar are fine; activity is judged from the posts themselves.
- Founder-reported revenue is a signal, not a fact: say "reports".
- Put every page a named place, rule or competitor claim came from in `docs/9-GO-TO-MARKET.md` → Sources, with the date checked.
- No web search available: say so, ask the member for the communities, creators and competitors they know, and mark those **[member, unverified]**.
