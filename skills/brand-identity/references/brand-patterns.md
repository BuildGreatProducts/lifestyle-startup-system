# Brand patterns

Category defaults to break away from, and the patterns that do it. Read in `brand-identity` step 2 to name the default, and in steps 3–5 when critiquing drift. The shapes are durable; the named examples date fast, so confirm a brand still looks this way before quoting it to the member. If web search isn't available, use these as priors and say they're unverified.

## Defaults by kind of business

Members turn a skill into a product, so the category is often a service niche before it's software. These are the looks a researched competitor set usually converges on — check the member's actual competitors before naming one.

| Kind of business | The usual default | Why it fails |
|---|---|---|
| Coaching, wellness, therapy | Sage or terracotta, a script or thin serif, smiling stock portraits, "your journey" copy | Every practitioner looks identical; the customer can't tell expertise apart |
| Consultancy, productized service | Navy and teal, a geometric sans, handshake or laptop stock, "solutions" copy | Reads as an agency that sells hours, which is what the product is trying not to be |
| Creator digital products (templates, courses, guides) | Pastel gradients, the same Canva template, a mockup on a marble desk | The product looks like its free competitors |
| AI products and agent tools | Purple gradient, Inter, abstract waves or 3D blobs; or light grey on black "minimal" | The most saturated look of the decade; invisible by design |
| Developer tools | Dark mode, monospace, neon accent, terminal screenshots | Fine when the UI is beautiful; otherwise indistinguishable |
| Local and trade services | Clip-art icon, primary colours, a stretched logo | Signals "small" when the offer is premium |

## Patterns that distinguish

- **Documentary photography of the customer's real world.** Patagonia, Tracksmith, Airbnb. Works when the product produces a real outcome the customer shows off — often true for a service.
- **A hand-drawn illustration system.** Notion, Stripe, Mailchimp. Humanises a technical product. Notion's references are Penguin paperback covers; Stripe's are Swiss design and the Whole Earth Catalog.
- **Pure UI, no people.** Linear, Arc, Raycast. Product as hero — only when the UI earns it.
- **Editorial / magazine layouts.** Type-led, denser than the usual SaaS page (Stripe's annual letter). Fits brands positioned on authority — often the right call for an expert's product.
- **A borrowed world from outside the category.** Heavy-metal posters for water (Liquid Death), apothecary labels for skincare (Aesop), field guides, transit signage, seed packets. The furthest reference usually carries the most distinctiveness.

## Contrarian-belief patterns

- **Reject the category's dominant assumption, name what you choose instead, give a reason.** "Most AI products believe the model is the product; we believe the workflow is, because models commoditise and workflows don't."
- **Sell the outcome the category sells by the hour.** "Most consultants sell time; we sell the finished deliverable at a fixed price."
- **Take the side of the customer's autonomy.** Granola: no bot in the meeting.

## Tone patterns

- **"X but not Y"** (Mailchimp) — every attribute excludes something.
- **Confident minimalism** (Linear) — short declaratives that respect the reader: "We rebuilt notifications. They're faster now."
- **Compassionate calm** (Headspace) — never urgent, lowers anxiety: "Take a deep breath. Just one."
- **Plain expert** — talks like the member on a good client call; specific numbers, no hedging. Often the most credible voice for a skill-based product.

## Calibration brands

| Brand | Tone | Look |
|---|---|---|
| Linear | Precise, calm, opinionated | Pure UI, dark mode, generous negative space |
| Granola | Calm, prosumer | Clean type, real screenshots, no abstract art |
| Notion | Calm, clear, reduces overwhelm | Hand-drawn illustration, page as canvas |
| Anthropic | Thoughtful, measured | Warm earth tones, custom illustration, type-led — deliberately not purple |
| Aesop | Literary, restrained | Apothecary labels, amber and cream, serif-led |
| Liquid Death | Punk, deadpan | Heavy-metal covers, horror posters, can as hero |

## Why these fail

- **"Like the leader, but ours."** Looking like the category leader makes the member the cheaper version of them.
- **A direction picked from a trend report.** It dates in a year and looks like everyone who read the same report.
- **A look that fights the belief.** A rebel belief in a corporate template tells the customer the belief isn't real.
