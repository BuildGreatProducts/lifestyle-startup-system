# Customer research

How to research the customer before drafting the Customer section, so it names real tools, communities, prices and words instead of a guess. Run it fresh every time — communities move, tools change, prices rise. Bring back **8–12 concrete data points**, each labelled **[member]** or **[researched]** with its link, and note which still need confirming with real customers.

## Start with the member's own evidence

A member productizing their skill usually has the best customer research already, and doesn't know it. Ask for it before searching:

- **Past and current clients** — who paid, what for, how they found the member. The segment that pays most often, with least hassle, is the first wedge.
- **The words clients used** — enquiry emails, intake forms, discovery-call notes, DMs, reviews. Pain lines in the client's own words beat anything a search returns.
- **What clients ask for that the member doesn't sell** — often the product itself.
- **Who the member turns away**, and why — the first draft of **Not for**.

For a for-yourself-first route, the member is customer zero: walk the same fields with them, then look for lookalikes in the places below.

## Then research the world

| Look for | Where | Bring back |
|---|---|---|
| **Role and situation** | Job postings for the role (they list duties and tools), "day in the life" posts and videos, the role's subreddit | Typical title, team size, when and where the problem shows up |
| **Pain in their own words** | Subreddits, forums, Facebook groups, Discord servers; 1–3 star reviews of the tools they use now (G2, Capterra, app stores, Trustpilot); podcast and YouTube comments | Verbatim quotes with links — these become Pain and Proof material |
| **What they use today** | Comparison and alternatives sites, "what do you use for X?" threads, shared spreadsheet templates, the job postings above | 3–5 named tools, the workaround, and how long people stay with it |
| **What they pay** | The current tools' pricing pages; what humans charge for the same job (freelance marketplaces, agency and consultant rate pages) | Named anchors with prices — hand off to [pricing-research.md](pricing-research.md) for the anchors themselves |
| **Where they gather** | Subreddit search, Discord and Slack community directories, Facebook group search, newsletter directories, podcast charts for the niche, conference speaker lists | Named communities with rough size and activity, named newsletters and podcasts, named people they trust |
| **What makes them buy or balk** | "Is X worth it?" threads, cancellation complaints, procurement or compliance requirements in the niche | The killer objection, and the proof that would answer it |

Useful search patterns: `site:reddit.com "[role]" "[task]"`, `"how do you" [task] [role]`, `"[current tool]" alternative`, `"[current tool]" "cancelled"`, `"[role]" newsletter`, `"[role]" podcast`.

## Trust and citing

- Open the page; a search snippet isn't a source. Quote exactly and link the thread or review.
- One loud thread is an anecdote. Look for the same pain in three places before calling it the pain.
- Community size and activity change: say "about 40K members, posts daily (checked <date>)", not "the biggest community".
- Anything the member says about clients is **[member]**; anything found is **[researched]**. A best guess that neither supports is tagged `[to confirm with customers]` — a normal output, not a failure.
- Every page used goes in `docs/2-OFFER.md` → Sources.
- No web search available: say so, lean on the member's client evidence, and label researched-style claims **[member, unverified]**.
