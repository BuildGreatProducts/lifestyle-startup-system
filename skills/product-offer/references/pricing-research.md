# Pricing research

How to find 2–3 real price anchors and the current platform fees for the shape, so the launch price sits against today's numbers rather than remembered ones. Run it fresh every time — an outdated anchor produces an outdated price. Never anchor against a free tool; anchor against what the customer already **pays** for.

## What makes a good anchor

An anchor is something the customer already pays for, or would compare this to. Look for one of each where they exist:

1. **The human alternative** — what a freelancer, agency, consultant or the member themselves charges for the same outcome. For a member productizing a skill, their own current rate is the first anchor, and usually the strongest.
2. **The direct competitor** — a product doing the same job for the same customer.
3. **The adjacent tool** — something in the customer's stack that sets their sense of "normal" for software in this budget.

For each, write down where the member's product sits and why: it replaces the anchor, does one slice of it better, or serves a smaller buyer.

## Where to look

| Anchor | Where | Bring back |
|---|---|---|
| Human alternative | The member's own rate card and invoices; freelance marketplace listings (Upwork, Fiverr, Contra); agency and consultant pricing pages; "how much should I charge for [service]" threads | A named price for a defined scope |
| Competitor | The product's own pricing page (open it; don't trust a review site's copy); store listings for in-app price tiers; storefronts (Gumroad, Etsy, Notion and Figma marketplaces) for digital products | Plan name, price, billing unit, what's included, and the entry route (trial, free tier) |
| Adjacent tool | Pricing pages of the 3–5 tools named in the Customer section | Price per seat or account per month |
| Price history | The Internet Archive's Wayback Machine on a pricing page | Whether prices are rising, and when plans changed |
| What people actually pay | "Is [tool] worth it?" and "what do you pay for" threads; review sites mentioning price; founder revenue posts | Complaints about price, discounts people get, what made them upgrade |

## Platform fees for the shape

Before the cost floor, check today's fees for how this shape collects money ([shapes.md](shapes.md) says which apply): card processing, app-store commission and its small-business rate, merchant-of-record fees, storefront and marketplace fees, freelance-marketplace service fees, per-message chat-platform fees, model provider prices per typical job. Bring back the figure and the page it came from; record it as a cost-floor assumption.

## Trust and citing

- Primary sources only: the pricing page, the listing, the rate card. Note the date checked next to every price.
- "Contact sales" is a signal (the buyer is an organisation with procurement), not a price — find a public figure elsewhere or say there isn't one.
- Compare like for like: per seat vs per account, monthly vs annual, the plan the customer would actually pick.
- Founder-reported revenue is a signal, not a fact: write "reports".
- Every anchor and fee goes in `docs/2-OFFER.md` → Sources.
- No web search available: say so, ask the member for prices they know, and label them **[member, unverified]**.
