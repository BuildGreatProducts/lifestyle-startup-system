# Idea audit

The structure `idea-finder` works through with the member in conversation: the inventory categories, the scoring axes and the gate, with `> Good/Bad` calibration for each answer. Nothing here is written into; the result goes to `docs/1-IDEA.md`. Each answer is one or two sentences unless the prompt says otherwise.

## 1. Starting Point

*What are you bringing — a business, expertise, a passion, or a mix? For a business: what you sell, who pays, and what it costs. For a passion: what you do, and the hours and money you already spend on it.*

> Good: a named service and a real price and customer count, or a named hobby with real hours and spend
> Bad: a mission statement, or "I love X"


---

## 2. Where the Time Goes

*What does a typical week look like — working hours or free hours — and what's the one thing that jams: the bottleneck stopping you doubling revenue without doubling hours, or the frustration that comes back every time you do the thing?*

> Good: hours against named activities, one named constraint or frustration
> Bad: "I'm just really busy"


---

## 3. Idea Inventory

*Concrete instances only — things you already do, know, own, or keep coming back to. Skip a lens that doesn't apply (write "Not applicable"); if a category is empty, say so.*

### Business & expertise lens

#### Repeated manual processes

*What do you do at least weekly that has the same shape every time?*


#### Spreadsheet-shaped work

*What currently runs in spreadsheets, docs, email chains, or WhatsApp threads?*


#### Judgment calls only you can make

*What decisions do clients pay for that live entirely in your head?*


#### Unique data or access

*What records, benchmarks, price lists, or insider positions do you have that competitors can't reach?*


#### What clients keep asking for

*What do customers repeatedly request that you don't sell — declined or handled ad hoc?*


#### The capacity bottleneck

*The single constraint from Section 2, examined properly: what exactly jams, and how often?*


### Passion lens

#### Recurring frustrations

*What keeps going wrong, every time you (and people like you) do the thing?*


#### Money already spent

*What do you and others in this world already pay for — gear, apps, coaching, events, memberships?*


#### Homemade workarounds

*What spreadsheets, Discord bots, forum guides, or templates have you or the community built because nothing good exists?*


#### Communities you're inside

*Where are you known and trusted — the forums, clubs, Discords, or events that would be your first users?*


---

## 4. Idea Shortlist

*3–5 candidates, one sentence each: who uses it, what it replaces, and which inventory item above it comes from.*

> Good: every idea traceable to a named inventory item
> Bad: an idea you could have had without running this audit


---

## 5. Scores

*Score each shortlisted idea 1–3 per axis, total out of 15. Ties break on distribution advantage — the axis you uniquely control. Staying power asks whether you'd still be working on it in two years if it earned little for a while; it never outweighs willingness to pay. An idea with no evidence that anyone pays today, in money or hours, can't be chosen, whatever its total — note why here. Evidence is a concrete data point (a price, an invoice, a spend, a count of hours, a public workaround), labelled [member] or [researched]. An idea for the people around you needs at least one data point showing that people other than you pay.*

## 6. The Route

*For yourself first, or for the people around you — who feels the pain most: you, or your clients or fellow enthusiasts?*

> Good: one route, with the reason
> Bad: "both, eventually"

## 7. The One Idea (Idea Brief)

*The chosen idea, stated as `product-offer`'s intake: the product in 1–2 sentences, and who it's for today.*

> Good: hands straight into `product-offer` with nothing left to translate
> Bad: still three ideas wearing a trenchcoat
